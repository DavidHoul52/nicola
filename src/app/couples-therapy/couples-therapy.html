Couples therapy

A space for both of you to be heard, understand the patterns between you, and find new ways of relating.

Working with couples

I’m particularly interested in how couples can become caught in painful patterns, how communication breaks down, and how understandable attempts to protect ourselves or have our needs met can sometimes unintentionally make things worse.

During the first four sessions, we work together to understand these patterns and draw them out on paper. This provides a kind of route map for the therapy that follows.

Therapy can provide an opportunity to slow these patterns down and understand what is happening between you. We can explore how you communicate, what you each need and how you might respond differently. An important part of this can be learning to repair ruptures — those moments when we hurt, disappoint or misunderstand each other — rather than allowing them to become increasingly entrenched.

I have specific training in Couple Therapy for Depression through Tavistock Relationships. The training was integrative, drawing on both Emotion-Focused Therapy and Systemic Therapy. Emotion-Focused Therapy explores the conscious and unconscious patterns we learn for managing emotions, which can sometimes inadvertently disrupt relationships and make it difficult to move forward. Systemic Therapy considers how people, their relationships, and the wider systems they are part of — including family, community, culture, and social structures — influence one another. I draw on these principles alongside CBT when working with couples experiencing a range of difficulties.

I also have a particular interest in estrangement and the complex emotional and relational issues that can arise when important relationships become distant or break down.

Is couples therapy right for us?

Couples therapy can help whether you want to strengthen your relationship, get through a difficult period, or think carefully about whether to stay together. You don’t need to agree on everything — or even on the problem — to begin.

If there is violence or abuse in the relationship, or one of you feels afraid of the other, couples therapy isn’t usually the right first step. Please mention this when you get in touch, and we can think about what would help.

If you’d rather start on your own, I also offer individual therapy — see how I can help.
