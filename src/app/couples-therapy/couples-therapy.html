Working with couples

An inclusive, supportive space

I offer an LGBTQIA+ affirmative approach to couples therapy, aiming to provide a respectful, welcoming and non-judgemental space. I recognise and value the diversity of people’s identities, backgrounds and experiences, and seek to understand each couple and their relationship without making assumptions or judgements.

Understanding relationship patterns

Relationships can bring great fulfilment, but also periods of difficulty, disconnection and distress. Couples can sometimes become caught in recurring patterns where communication breaks down and attempts to feel heard, understood or reassured lead to further conflict or distance.

Together, we can slow things down and explore what’s happening beneath the surface. This can help you develop a deeper understanding of your own feelings and needs, as well as those of your partner.

Finding new ways forward

We can work towards more constructive ways of communicating, managing disagreements and finding solutions together. An important part of this is learning how to reconnect after moments of hurt or misunderstanding, repair difficulties and rebuild trust and emotional closeness.

My approach

I work impartially and collaboratively, without taking sides or seeking to establish who is right or wrong. At times, I may gently challenge one or both partners, drawing attention to patterns or dynamics that can be difficult to recognise from within the relationship. My aim is to help you understand each other more fully and explore different ways of relating.

What can we work on?

Couples come to therapy for many reasons, including ongoing conflict, communication difficulties, anxiety or stress within the relationship, and the demands of family life. Therapy can also help you navigate significant life changes, such as becoming parents, bereavement, separation or other transitions that place pressure on a relationship.

We can also explore sexual difficulties and their impact on intimacy, communication and emotional closeness. This can form part of our wider couples work, although it is distinct from specialist psychosexual therapy, which focuses more specifically on sexual functioning and difficulties.

Is couples therapy right for us?

Couples therapy can help whether you want to strengthen your relationship, get through a difficult period, or think carefully about whether to stay together. You don’t need to agree on everything — or even on the problem — to begin.

Couples therapy is not appropriate in every circumstance. Where there is ongoing coercive control, domestic violence or active addiction, including difficulties relating to substances, pornography or gambling, a different form of specialist support may be more appropriate. In these circumstances, the safety and wellbeing of each partner need to be considered first.

If you’d rather start on your own, I also offer individual therapy — see how I can help.
