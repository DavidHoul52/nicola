Working with couples

Relationships can be a source of great fulfilment, but they can also bring periods of difficulty, disconnection and distress. Couples can sometimes find themselves caught in recurring patterns of interaction, where communication becomes strained and attempts to feel heard, understood or reassured can unintentionally lead to further conflict or distance.

Working together we can slow things down and make sense of the patterns that unfold between you. We can explore what may be happening beneath the surface of your difficulties, helping each of you to develop a deeper understanding of your own feelings and needs, as well as those of your partner. Where helpful, we can also work towards developing more constructive ways of communicating, managing disagreements and finding solutions together.

An important aspect of this work is learning how to reconnect after moments of hurt, disappointment or misunderstanding. Rather than allowing these experiences to create lasting distance, therapy can help you find ways to acknowledge and repair difficulties, rebuild trust and strengthen your emotional connection.

My approach as a couples therapist is impartial and collaborative. I do not take sides or seek to establish who is right or wrong. However, I may at times gently challenge one or both partners, drawing attention to patterns or dynamics that may be difficult to recognise from within the relationship, but which could be contributing to dissatisfaction or distress. My aim is to help you understand each other more fully and consider different ways of relating.

Couples come to therapy for many different reasons. You may be experiencing ongoing conflict, communication difficulties, anxiety or stress within your relationship, or finding it difficult to navigate the demands of family life. Therapy can also offer support during significant life transitions, such as becoming parents, bereavement, separation or other changes that place new pressures on a relationship.

We can also explore the impact of sexual difficulties on your relationship, including how these may affect intimacy, communication and emotional closeness. These issues can be considered as part of our wider therapeutic work, although this is distinct from specialist psychosexual therapy, which focuses more specifically on sexual functioning and difficulties.

Is couples therapy right for us?

Couples therapy can help whether you want to strengthen your relationship, get through a difficult period, or think carefully about whether to stay together. You don’t need to agree on everything — or even on the problem — to begin.

Couples therapy is not appropriate in every circumstance. Where there is ongoing coercive control, domestic violence or active addiction, including difficulties relating to substances, pornography or gambling, a different form of specialist support may be more appropriate. In these circumstances, the safety and wellbeing of each partner need to be considered first.

If you’d rather start on your own, I also offer individual therapy — see how I can help.
