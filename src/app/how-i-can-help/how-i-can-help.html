How I can help

What is CBT?

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is a practical, collaborative therapy. It looks at how our thoughts, feelings, body and behaviour affect one another, and how patterns that once made sense can keep difficulties going now.

Alongside what is happening in the present, we also make sense of where those patterns came from. Then, together, we try out new ways of relating to yourself, others and the world.

What you can expect

Both individual and couples CBT are time-limited therapies, typically lasting a maximum of 20 weeks.

The aim is for you to become your own therapist by developing the knowledge, skills and confidence to use CBT strategies independently in everyday life. This can make therapy more affordable while also promoting greater autonomy and empowerment.

Following therapy, you may benefit from a period of consolidation to practise and embed your skills. If needed, you can return for further therapy after this period.

Therapy is a collaborative process. I’ll draw on my experience and knowledge to help you understand what is happening, while working with you to find an approach that feels meaningful and useful.

I continue to learn and read widely, drawing on ideas from different therapeutic traditions. I’m interested in what helps people make lasting changes, develop greater self-understanding and compassion, and build relationships that feel more connected and workable.

My aim is for therapy to be a space where you can feel understood, think about things differently and explore possibilities for change — with someone who brings both professional expertise and genuine human curiosity.

Individual or couples therapy

I work with individuals and with couples. If the difficulties you’re facing are centred on your relationship, couples therapy gives you both space to be heard and to understand the patterns between you.
